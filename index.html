Abstract

Augmented dexterity promises to relieve surgeon workload and improve surgical performance, yet it still requires expert algorithmic engineering for each manipulation subtask. Can emerging coding agents deliver augmented dexterity across subtasks, while remaining reliable under failure? In this work, we introduce SARA (Surgical Agent for Resilient Autonomy), an agentic surgical robotics framework that executes diverse manipulation subtasks autonomously and resiliently. Given a nominal task graph composed of reusable surgical skills, SARA proactively anticipates how the skills may fail and induces those failures in a physics-based sandbox. From the induced and naturally occurring failures, it writes observable verifiers for the skills and proposes failure-specific recovery strategies, either by re-executing upstream skills of the nominal graph or by escalating to the broader skill library. SARA then rehearses the candidate augmented graph in the sandbox, refining the ineffective strategies in a self-improvement loop, and retains only the validated verifiers and recoveries in the final augmented graph. We evaluate SARA on 8 surgical subtasks in Newton-based simulation and 6 subtasks on a physical da Vinci robot.
